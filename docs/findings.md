# Findings: Mint Velvet (Sabre Retail Fashion Ltd), FY22-FY25

Independent analysis of public filings. Figures in GBP; page references are to the filed PDFs in `data/raw/` (pdf page; printed page in `docs/source_registry.md`).

**1. Each pound of lost sales cost about two pounds of operating profit.** Turnover fell 6.2% from FY22 to FY24 (GBP 143.7m to GBP 134.7m) while PBT fell 53.7% (GBP 30.7m to GBP 14.2m). Each GBP 1 of lost turnover cost GBP 2.02 of operating profit in FY23 and GBP 1.87 in FY24, because costs kept rising as sales fell. In FY25 turnover rose 11.5% to GBP 150.2m and PBT 41.9% to GBP 20.2m: GBP 0.40 of operating profit per extra GBP 1 of sales. (P&L: FY23 accounts p.17, FY24 p.17, FY25 p.18.)

**2. The fall came from the 'other operating costs' pool, not payroll or rent.** Costs outside staff, rent, D&A and FX rose from 62.6% of turnover in FY22 to 72.2% in FY24. The rate change took GBP 5.4m off PBT in FY23 (77% of a GBP 7.0m fall) and GBP 7.7m in FY24 (81% of GBP 9.5m); it added back GBP 4.5m in FY25 as the ratio eased to 69.2%. This is not just fixed costs spread over lower sales: the pool grew by GBP 7.3m in absolute terms from FY22 to FY24 while turnover fell by GBP 8.9m. Staff costs moved by less than GBP 1m in either falling year. The filings do not split this pool. FY22 is the only year that discloses cost of stock (29.0% of turnover, a 71.0% product margin; FY22 p.34), so the rest of the pool (concession commissions, marketing, distribution and similar) was 33.6% of turnover that year. The FY24 strategic report gives concession sales -13.5% and owned-channel mix +2.9pp (FY24 p.4). That fits a mix shift toward online, with its fulfilment and returns costs, but it does not prove one.

**3. Payroll is now the main cost headwind.** FY25 staff costs rose 19.1% (GBP 18.1m to GBP 21.5m) against 11.5% turnover growth; average store staff rose from 623 to 692 (FY25 p.34). Employer social security rose to 9.6% of wages from 8.2% in FY24 as originally filed (FY24 p.33). The FY25 filing restates the FY24 split to 6.8%; see `docs/discrepancies.md`. The April 2025 employer NIC change (15% above GBP 5,000, previously 13.8% above GBP 9,100) covered about 38 of FY25's 52 weeks, so FY26 carries the full-year effect (inference). For a 20-hour associate at NLW plus 5%, employer NIC rises from GBP 891 to GBP 1,583 a year (78%).

**4. Stock built up as sales fell, then ran down.** Year-end stock rose 46% to GBP 16.7m in FY24 while turnover fell 3.6%: cover went from 4.3 to 6.5 weeks of turnover. In FY25 stock was GBP 15.0m (5.2 weeks) on 11.5% higher sales. The obsolescence provision reached GBP 1.23m, 7.6% of gross stock, up from 4.5% in FY22 (FY25 p.32, p.40; FY24 p.19).

**5. Against the market: behind in FY23, ahead in FY25.** Turnover growth against the ONS clothing and footwear stores value index (GB): FY23 -2.7% vs +7.6% (-10.3ppt); FY24 -3.6% vs -2.2% (-1.4ppt); FY25 +11.5% vs +3.2% (+8.3ppt). The comparison is indicative: calendar-year averages are set against 52-week fiscal years, and the ONS category average mixes value and premium retailers.

**New-store appraisal (illustrative).** Base costs: a 1,700 sq ft regional-mall unit needs mature sales of GBP 424 per sq ft a year (GBP 0.72m) to return the 10% hurdle over a 10-year lease. Downside costs (rent GBP 200,000, fit-out GBP 931,500, product margin 66%, slower ramp-up) raise that to GBP 677 per sq ft. The base case implies a 38% four-wall margin at maturity. If internal store P&Ls run lower, the gap most likely sits in the two inputs to replace first: other store opex (an assumed 10% of sales) and rent (an estate-average proxy). The inputs are labelled assumptions or filing proxies (`config/assumptions.yaml`). These are not Mint Velvet's store economics.

**What the data cannot show.** There is no store, channel or like-for-like split, and no product margin after FY22. There is no budget, so there is no variance against budget or forecast. There is no marketing spend, so there is no marketing ROI. Note 2.6 puts all retail-channel costs in cost of sales, so the reported gross margin (FY22 35.1%, FY25 26.7%) is a channel contribution margin, not a product margin.
